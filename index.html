How OKX promo codes work behind the scenes

Every OKX user can invite others, and every invite has a code. When a new account registers with that code, OKX pays the inviter a commission out of the trading fees the new user generates. The inviter can then pass part of that commission back to the new user. That passed-back part is the "kickback" you see when you type a code into the sign-up form.

That's why OKX promo codes aren't all the same. Two codes can show two different kickback rates, depending on how the inviter set them up. It also explains why the form shows the rate right away: OKX wants you to know what you're getting before you register. With 1888802, check the message under the field and go ahead once you see the discount.

Notice what doesn't happen. The inviter doesn't see your balance, your trades or your personal data. They see aggregated commission figures, and that's all. Your account belongs only to you.

Five ways new users lose their promo benefits

  1. Registering first, reading later. The referral line is marked optional and sits below the main form, so it's easy to skip. Once you've pressed Sign up, there's no going back.
  2. Mixing devices. Opening the link on a laptop and finishing on a phone app often loses the code. Finish the whole sign-up in one place.
  3. Leaving KYC for "later". Many reward tasks only start counting after verification. Users who verify three weeks in find half of the tasks already expired.
  4. Ignoring voucher expiry dates. Fee vouchers and trial bonuses usually have a short life. Check the Rewards Center within your first few days.
  5. Trading only with market orders. The kickback lowers your fees, but limit orders that add liquidity are cheaper still. Use both savings together.
